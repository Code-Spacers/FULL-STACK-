The goal

Six pillars of a sustainable city.

Sustainable Development Goal 11 calls for inclusive, safe, resilient, and sustainable cities. NIVARA translates those targets into design decisions people can actually live with.

Adequate housing

Safe, affordable dwellings with basic services — the foundation of Target 11.1.

Public transport

Accessible, affordable mobility that cuts congestion and expands opportunity (11.2).

Inclusive public space

Parks, streets, and civic rooms that work for children, elders, and people with disabilities (11.7).

Lower environmental impact

Cleaner air, careful water use, and waste systems that treat the city as a living metabolism (11.6).

Disaster resilience

Prepared communities, early warning, and infrastructure that can absorb climate shocks (11.5, 11.b).

Sustainable planning

Participatory, integrated urban planning so growth does not outrun nature or equity (11.3).

Solutions

Practical technology with civic consequence.

Each proposal pairs a familiar urban problem with technology already used in cities worldwide.

Solar-powered streetlights

  1. Problem: Dark corridors after grid failure or delayed maintenance.
  2. Technology: Photovoltaic canopies with battery storage and occupancy dimming.
  3. Environment: Cuts night-time grid draw and avoids diesel backup.
  4. Community: Safer walking routes, especially for shift workers and students.

Intelligent waste collection

  1. Problem: Overflowing bins and trucks running empty routes.
  2. Technology: Fill-level sensing and route software for municipal fleets.
  3. Environment: Fewer unnecessary vehicle kilometres and less leakage into drains.
  4. Community: Cleaner markets and fewer pest-related complaints.

Smart traffic management

  1. Problem: Peak congestion that stalls buses as well as private cars.
  2. Technology: Adaptive signals with transit priority, not only camera enforcement.
  3. Environment: Smoother flow reduces idling emissions at junctions.
  4. Community: More reliable bus times make car-free commuting realistic.

Rainwater harvesting

  1. Problem: Storm runoff floods streets while households still buy tanker water.
  2. Technology: Roof catchments, first-flush filters, and recharge pits at plot and ward scale.
  3. Environment: Recharges local aquifers and eases pressure on distant sources.
  4. Community: More reliable non-potable water for gardens, flushing, and cooling.

Electric public transportation

  1. Problem: Diesel buses concentrate pollution on corridors poorest riders use.
  2. Technology: Depot charging, opportunity charging, and bus-priority lanes.
  3. Environment: Zero tailpipe emissions on the route; grid mix still matters.
  4. Community: Quieter streets and cleaner air at stops and terminals.

Green rooftops

  1. Problem: Heat islands and stormwater spikes on sealed roofs.
  2. Technology: Extensive sedum or intensive gardens with waterproofing layers.
  3. Environment: Cools surfaces and delays runoff during intense rain.
  4. Community: Shared amenity space where structure and access allow it.

Pedestrian-friendly streets

  1. Problem: Discontinuous sidewalks force people into traffic.
  2. Technology: Continuous footways, kerb cuts, shade trees, and 30 km/h design speeds.
  3. Environment: Shorter car trips when walking becomes the default.
  4. Community: Independent mobility for children and older residents.

Citizen-powered civic reporting

  1. Problem: Issues stay invisible until they become emergencies.
  2. Technology: Structured digital reports with photos, location, and a reference ID.
  3. Environment: Faster repair of leaks, dumps, and failed lights reduces risk.
  4. Community: A documented loop between residents and works departments.

Impact map

Features aligned to SDG 11 targets.

11.1

Safer, more inclusive cities

Civic reporting and lit, walkable streets make hazards visible and fixable, supporting adequate services in every neighbourhood.

11.2

Sustainable transport

Live mobility KPIs, electric buses, and signal priority treat public transport as the city's backbone rather than leftover space.

11.3

Participatory planning

Residents see the same picture planners use — a rehearsal for Target 11.3's inclusive, participatory urban planning.

11.6

Reduced environmental impact

Air, waste, water, and energy tiles make urban metabolism visible so environmental load can be discussed with numbers.

11.7

Green and public space

Urban forest and green-roof proposals expand accessible public space, including for women, children, older persons, and persons with disabilities.

11.b

Resilience to disasters

Rainwater systems, canopy, and status monitoring are framed as climate-adaptation infrastructure, not decoration.

About NIVARA

An urban innovation studio for SDG 11.

NIVARA is an academic exhibition platform showing how design, data literacy, and civic tools can support Sustainable Cities and Communities. It does not operate municipal services.
